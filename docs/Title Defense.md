## **1\. Introduction:**

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most prevalent endocrine disorders among women, with global frequencies ranging from 8% to 12% depending on the diagnostic criteria applied \[1, 4, 7\]. Despite its prevalence, most cases remain undetected due to the complexity of the diagnostic pipeline. No single test can definitively confirm PCOS; clinicians must rely on a combination of hormonal blood panels and ovarian ultrasound,  a process that is time-consuming and heavily operator-dependent \[5, 11, 13\]. The Rotterdam Consensus Criteria, which requires polycystic ovarian morphology as one of three diagnostic conditions, is inconsistently applied, and ultrasound images are inherently difficult to interpret due to speckle noise, low contrast, and variable acquisition technique \[8\]. False positive and negative diagnoses lead to inappropriate treatments that worsen patient health outcomes \[11\].

Deep learning,  particularly transfer-learned CNNs,  has achieved notable classification accuracy in automated PCOS detection, frequently exceeding 95% on benchmark datasets \[1, 3, 7, 8, 25\]. However, accuracy alone does not guarantee clinical trustworthiness. Current models universally output a single SoftMax probability value that is mathematically established to be overconfident and uncalibrated \[22\]. No prior PCOS study has formally quantified Expected Calibration Error or applied MC Dropout-based uncertainty quantification, leaving clinicians without a principled basis for trusting model confidence scores \[16, 19\]. Furthermore, prior work evaluates architectures against fixed preprocessing pipelines, leaving the interaction between preprocessing choice and model performance systematically unexplored \[4\].

This study proposes a comprehensive, trust-oriented deep learning pipeline for PCOS detection that incorporates calibration analysis, MC Dropout uncertainty quantification, an uncertainty-gated clinical referral system, and multi-method XAI, benchmarked on the publicly available Figshare PCOS Ultrasound Dataset \[26\].

## **2\. Background Study:**

**Clinical and Diagnostic Foundations:** Polycystic Ovary Syndrome is classified as a heterogeneous endocrine disorder that is characterized by reproductive, metabolic and psychological manifestation. Rotterdam Criteria (2003) being the most widely adopted diagnostic criteria for PCOS detection, entails the presence of at least 2 of three of the following features as a requirement to classify a case as PCOS:

(1) Oligo-ovulation or anovulation, 

(2) Clinical and/or biochemical signs of hyperandrogenism, and 

(3) Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound imaging, defined as 12 or more follicles measuring 2-9mm in diameter or increased ovarian volume exceeding 10ml or 10 cm³ \[2\].

**Image Preprocessing Techniques for Medical Ultrasound Enhancement:** 

Contrast Enhancement: Adaptive histogram equalization methods, particularly Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE), enhance local contrast by computing histograms on small image tiles and redistributing intensity values.

Noise Reduction: Speckle noise, inherent to ultrasound imaging due to wave interference, can obscure fine follicular structures. Gaussian filtering, bilateral filtering (which preserves edges while smoothing homogeneous regions), wavelet-based denoising techniques and most importantly anisotropic diffusion effectively reduce noise while maintaining structural details essential for accurate classification \[14\].

**Uncertainty Quantification & Model Calibration:** Neural networks output a SoftMax probability vector at their final layer. These values are often mistaken as calibrated confidence scores. Modern CNNs are poorly calibrated. This leads to overconfident behavior despite unreliability \[22\]. The ECE acts as a quantifiable measure of this miscalibration through measuring absolute difference between predicted confidence and observed accuracy across confidence bins. ECE values typically exist in 4-10% range for state of the art models and the single most effective way that consistently outperforms all other calibration methods is temperature scaling adjustment \[22\]. To be noted that temperature scaling is a post training correction mechanism that divides the logits by a learned scaler, T.

MC dropout addresses a correlated but a dissimilar issue, which is model uncertainty. During training dropout randomly turns  off neurons to prevent overfitting. By keeping the dropout active while passing the same input through the network multiple times we'll get variance in prediction which serves as a principled estimate of model uncertainty, grounded in the theoretical relation between dropout-regularized networks and Bayesian approximate inference\[16\]. This technique produces predictive entropy scores for each input, high entropy scores convey genuine uncertainty while low corresponds to true confidence.

## **3\. Problem Statement:**

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome or PCOS for short is one of the most influential endocrine disorders affecting women. Global frequencies range from 8% to 12% based on a set of diagnostic criterion/s applied \[1, 4, 7\]. Yet most cases go undetected. Untreated PCOS correlates to infertility, insulin resistance, type-II diabetes, cardiovascular disease, endometriosis, endometrial cancer in addition to deteriorating mental health, such as depression and anxiety. \[7, 13\]. The diagnostic pipeline in itself is part of the problem. No single test can decisively detect or identify PCOS. Healthcare professionals rely on a combination of blood-based hormone panels and ovarian ultrasound. This is inefficient and time consuming to say the least while also being heavily reliant on professional expertise \[5, 11, 13\]. In spite of the subjective nature of transvaginal ultrasound, it is often preferred over other methods due to its relative effectiveness\[1\]. One other standard broadly accepted is the Rotterdam Consensus Criteria. This requires identifying polycystic structures in ovaries as one of 3 fulfilling conditions. Even then this is inconsistently applied. The ultrasound images themselves are also inherently hard to interpret due to issues such as noise, low contrast, positioning & operator technique \[8\]. False positive and false negative cases cause inappropriate treatments and delay that lead to worsening of overall patient health condition \[11\]. This diagnostic inadequacy provides a clear need for automated clinically trustworthy tools.

## **4\. Preliminary Novelty Statement:**

This study presents five interconnected contributions that collectively address gaps unresolved in prior PCOS detection literature. First, it conducts the first systematic benchmark of nine CNN architectures across six preprocessing conditions,  including the first application of Perona-Malik anisotropic diffusion in place of Gaussian filtering, which more accurately models multiplicative ultrasound speckle noise,  on the public Figshare PCOS Ultrasound Dataset. Second, it performs the first formal calibration analysis of PCOS detection models, quantifying Expected Calibration Error (ECE) before and after temperature scaling correction across all nine architectures. Third, it introduces Monte Carlo Dropout-based uncertainty quantification to PCOS detection for the first time, producing per-case predictive entropy scores that distinguish genuinely confident predictions from uncertain ones. Fourth, it designs an uncertainty-gated clinical referral system,  a coverage-accuracy framework enabling the model to autonomously classify confident cases while routing ambiguous cases to clinical review. Fifth, it integrates three complementary XAI methods (Grad-CAM, Layer-wise Relevance Propagation, and SHAP DeepExplainer) and connects their outputs to the uncertainty analysis to characterise model behaviour on borderline cases.

## **5\. Research Questions:**

**RQ1:** How does preprocessing pipeline choice (across six conditions: raw, CLAHE-only, CLAHE+Gaussian, CLAHE+Anisotropic Diffusion, and each with Z-score normalisation) interact with CNN architecture performance across nine architectures for binary PCOS ultrasound classification?

**RQ2:** Can Monte Carlo Dropout uncertainty quantification, combined with temperature scaling calibration analysis, improve the clinical reliability of deep learning PCOS detection models beyond what single-point SoftMax confidence scores provide?

**RQ3:** Do multi-method XAI explanations (Grad-CAM, LRP, SHAP) produce clinically meaningful attributions aligned with known PCOS follicular morphology, and do high-uncertainty cases exhibit systematically different activation patterns from confident ones? 

## **6\. Objectives:**

The project pursues the following objectives:

**O1:** To benchmark nine pre-trained CNN architectures across six preprocessing conditions on the Figshare PCOS Ultrasound Dataset and identify the optimal architecture-preprocessing configuration.

**O2:** To implement Monte Carlo Dropout-based uncertainty quantification and design an uncertainty-gated clinical referral system with a coverage-accuracy trade-off analysis.

**O3:** To conduct the first formal calibration analysis in PCOS detection, measuring Expected Calibration Error before and after temperature scaling post-hoc correction.

**O4:** To apply and compare three XAI methods (Grad-CAM, LRP, SHAP DeepExplainer) and validate that model decisions are grounded in clinically relevant follicular morphology.

**O5:** To evaluate all models using seven metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, F1-score, AUC-ROC, and MCC) on a strictly held-out test set to ensure unbiased comparison.

## **7\. Expected Outcome:**

This study is expected to deliver the following outputs:

1. A comprehensive 9×6 preprocessing-architecture benchmark matrix reporting accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, F1-score, AUC-ROC, and MCC across all 54 training runs, enabling the selection of the optimal model configuration.  
2. Calibration analysis results,  including ECE scores and reliability diagrams before and after temperature scaling correction,  for all nine architectures, constituting the first published calibration study in PCOS detection.  
3. Per-case predictive entropy scores from MC Dropout (50 stochastic forward passes), along with a coverage-accuracy curve demonstrating the clinical utility of the uncertainty-gated referral system.  
4. Grad-CAM, LRP, and SHAP visualisations confirming that model attention is concentrated on clinically relevant ovarian follicular structures rather than imaging artefacts.  
5. A qualitative comparison of XAI activation maps between high-confidence and high-uncertainty cases, providing interpretable evidence for model reliability in borderline diagnostic scenarios.

## **8\. Scope of Work:**

This project is bounded by the following constraints and design decisions:

1. **Dataset:** Restricted to the publicly available Figshare PCOS Ultrasound Dataset (11,784 images, 6,784 PCOS and 5,000 non-PCOS); no external datasets are incorporated.  
2. **Classification:** Binary only (PCOS vs. non-PCOS); no severity staging, grading, or multiclass output is produced.  
3. **Modality:** Ovarian ultrasound images only; clinical tabular features (hormonal panels, BMI, etc.) are not incorporated into the model.  
4. **Architectures:** Nine pre-trained CNN architectures from four families (ResNet, DenseNet, EfficientNet, InceptionV3); no custom architectures are designed from scratch.  
5. **Preprocessing:** Six conditions tested per architecture: raw baseline, CLAHE-only, CLAHE \+ Gaussian filtering, CLAHE \+ Anisotropic Diffusion, and each of the latter two with Z-score normalisation.  
6. **Uncertainty:** MC Dropout only (50 stochastic forward passes at test time); Mean Field Variational Inference is excluded due to computational cost and lower accuracy.  
7. **XAI:** Applied to the best-performing model only, using Grad-CAM, LRP, and SHAP DeepExplainer.  
8. **Calibration:** Temperature scaling applied as a post-hoc correction on the validation set; no intra-training calibration methods are explored.  
9. **Validation:** No prospective clinical trial, patient study, or external multi-centre dataset validation is conducted.
